Personal project

5-DOF Robotic Arm

An Arduino-controlled arm with five degrees of freedom and a geared gripper, designed to reach 50 cm and lift a 110 g payload. Each joint is driven manually by its own potentiometer.

Status
In progress
Specs
50 cm reach, 110 g payload, 5 DOF plus gripper
Hardware
4 × DS3218 servos, 2 × SG90 servos, 6 potentiometers, Arduino Uno, 6 V 5 A supply
Tools
SolidWorks, 3D printing, Arduino, Tinkercad
Done so far
CAD complete, parts printing, electronics ordered, control code written

Why I'm building it

During my manufacturing internship at Racing Force Group, I watched plant workers spend hours on repetitive tasks like sorting screws. This arm is my first step toward automating that kind of work.

Challenges

Designing the gripper around its servo, accounting for range of motion and interference with the linkages, then laying out wiring where every servo responds reliably to its own potentiometer.

Next steps

Assemble the printed linkages and electronics, then test each joint and the gripper under load.

UW Autonomous Flight Team, Test Stand subteam

Gyroscopic Test Stand

I designed and manufactured the drone mounting plate and bearing subsystem for a 3-axis gyroscopic test stand, so the team can test flight patterns and tune flight controllers without risking a crash.

Status
Built and tested
My part
Drone mounting plate and bearing subsystem
Plate
3 mm carbon fiber, 16 plies of prepreg, press-cured and waterjet cut
Fits
4 drone designs: small quadcopter, package-delivery octocopter, high-speed drone, X-frame quadcopter
Tools
SolidWorks, heated platen press, waterjet, 3D printing
Result
Flight-tested an autonomous quadcopter on the stand, giving the team usable data for PID tuning

Challenges

One plate had to hold all four of the team's drone designs: a small quadcopter, a package-delivery octocopter, a high-speed drone and an X-frame quadcopter. I worked with each subteam on frame shape, size and mounting points, without compromising low-friction rotation on the stand.

What I learned

Designing for multi-fit compatibility, laying up and press-curing my own carbon fiber plate from prepreg, waterjet cutting, and assembling a subsystem that produces measurable results.

UW Human Powered Submarine, Hull and Drivetrain team

Composite Submarine Hull

I fabricated a fiberglass prototype to validate our layup technique, then manufactured the final carbon fiber tail cone by wet layup, following a NACA 65-025 profile.

Status
Raced
Profile
NACA 65-025
Mold
CNC-machined PET foam
Tools
SolidWorks, wet fiberglass and carbon layup, epoxy mixing, mold release, sanding
Result
3rd overall at the European International Submarine Races, top speed 2.21 knots

Challenges

Controlling resin distribution and keeping air pockets out of the layup, so no surface defects formed after curing.

What I learned

How to lay up carbon fiber and fiberglass structures while controlling resin, and how a prototype layup de-risks the final part.
